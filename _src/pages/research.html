How the modelling works

From a PESTLE scan to ten million simulations.

A decision worth modelling never hangs on three numbers. It starts with a structured sweep of everything that could move the outcome — political, economic, social, technological, legal and environmental — which on a serious engagement runs to several hundred individual factors.

Each one gets a range rather than a guess. Then the whole thing is simulated millions of times over, so the answer arrives as a distribution of outcomes with probabilities attached, and a shortlist of the few factors that genuinely decide it.

Read the full guide to the method {{SNIP:arrow}}

The four stages

  • Scan. A PESTLE sweep to surface every factor that could plausibly affect the decision — hundreds of them, deliberately, because the dangerous ones are the ones nobody thought to list.
  • Bound. Every factor given a realistic range instead of a point estimate, so the uncertainty stays in the model rather than being quietly discarded.
  • Simulate. Millions of runs — ten million on the largest engagement — each drawing a different combination, until the shape of the outcome stabilises.
  • Reduce. Sensitivity analysis strips it back to the handful of factors that actually move the answer. That shortlist is usually the most useful thing in the report.
