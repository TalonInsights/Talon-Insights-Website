01

Why it matters

Nobody wants a website. They want what a good one does.

Five outcomes, and everything I do exists to deliver one of them. The service is just the route.

  • Credibility, before you ever speak

    Most people check you online before they call. What they find either backs up the recommendation they were given, or quietly undermines it. A site built properly makes the decision easy.

  • Visibility where buying happens

    Being the best joiner in Telford is worth very little if the three names in the map pack are somebody else. Local SEO is the unglamorous work that changes that.

  • Enquiries, not just visits

    Traffic that doesn't turn into phone calls is a vanity metric. Every build is pointed at making it obvious what to do next and easy to do it — on one job the whole homepage became a single question.

  • Your time back, and fewer expensive mistakes

    Planning on spreadsheets costs hours every week and surfaces errors on delivery day. A tool built round your workflow catches them at the point of entry instead.

  • Decisions you can actually defend

    When the call is big enough to hurt, opinion isn't good enough. Research and modelling gives you a range, a probability, and the assumptions that decide it.

02

Why people call me

You've probably been let down by one of these.

Every one of them is avoidable. Most of them are why I run jobs the way I do.

  1. The bait and switch

    A senior person sells you the job, then a junior or an offshore team builds it. You never speak to the seller again.

  2. Lock-in

    You don't own your domain, your code or your content. Leaving means starting from nothing — and everybody knows it.

  3. A pretty site that does nothing

    It looks smart and generates no enquiries, because nobody asked what the business needed before designing it.

  4. No discovery

    Nobody came to see how you actually work. The quote was written from a ten-minute phone call and a guess.

  5. Vague quotes, moving numbers

    Everything you assumed was included turns out to be extra, and there's no written scope to point at.

  6. Support that vanishes

    It went live, then the emails stopped being answered. Nobody can explain why anything was built the way it was.
