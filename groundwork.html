Step zero — free

Start with a fifteen-minute phone call.

Nobody should be asked for money before they've spoken to me. So we start on the phone: what the business does, what's not working, and whether a Groundwork Visit is even the right next move. No charge, no obligation, no pitch.

I'd rather spend a quarter of an hour establishing that I'm not the right fit than take up half a day of yours reaching the same conclusion in person. If that's where it lands, I'll say so on the call and point you somewhere better.

Fifteen minutes, free, on the phone — and you leave knowing whether a visit is worth booking. No diary tennis, no travel, no obligation either way.

Then the visit

Three steps. One document — watch it happen.

A visit produces a written scope. As you read the steps, the document assembles itself the way it does on the day.

01

I come to you

Your unit, your office, your yard — anywhere in the West Midlands, at a time that suits you. An hour or two on site. No jargon: I explain everything in plain English, and I'm there to listen more than talk.

02

I map how you work

What a job looks like from enquiry to invoice. Where the time goes. Which parts hurt, which parts your people quietly work around, and which of them software or a better website can genuinely fix — often less than you'd fear.

03

You get it in writing

A short document within the week: what I recommend, what it would cost as a fixed price, what it deliberately excludes, and what I'd do first. The document alongside is the shape of it — yours gets written about your floor, not anybody else's.

Common questions

Asked before, answered straight.

Is it really free, start to finish?

Yes. A free call, then a free visit, then a written scope that's yours to keep. You pay for work, not for the conversation that decides whether there's any work worth doing.

What's the catch, if it's free?

There isn't one, but there is a reason. I'm early enough that a documented, well-run job matters more to me than a discovery fee — so I'd rather spend the time, do it properly, and let the written scope make the argument. If it doesn't, you've lost nothing but an hour.

What do you need from us on the day?

An hour or two of the owner's time, and ideally ten minutes with whoever actually runs the process day to day — they always know where the real problems are. No preparation needed. I'm coming to see how it works, not a rehearsal.

Is there any obligation afterwards?

None whatsoever. The worst outcome available is that you spend an hour or two of your time and end up with a written document telling you exactly what your business needs and what it should cost — before spending anything, with anybody.

How soon do I get the scope?

Within one week of the visit, usually sooner. If anything in it isn't clear, the follow-up call to walk through it costs nothing.
