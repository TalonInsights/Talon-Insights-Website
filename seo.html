SEO without the smoke.

No "guaranteed page one." No mystery reports. Local SEO is unglamorous, methodical work — done every month, measured honestly, and explained in plain English. It takes three to six months to show anything real, and anyone promising faster is selling you something else.

Show, don't tell

Most SEO is invisible. So here's an X-ray.

Every value below is lifted straight from this site's own homepage — the real title tag, the real alt text, the real schema — laid out as a simplified sketch rather than the page's exact design. Flip it to Googlebot view to see the layer search engines actually read, then press Ctrl+U on the real thing: every word is genuinely in the page.

Five of the levers, pinned to where they live

Flip the switch, or press any numbered pin. Nothing here is staged for the demo — each pin explains a decision that is live on this site right now, and tells you how to check it.

The search snippet is authored, not scraped

The blue link in a Google result is written here — a 51-character title so it never truncates, a description that ends on the call to action, and a canonical address so ranking signals never split between duplicate URLs. Every page carries its own, plus its own social preview card. verify: Ctrl+U → line 6

One H1, and it says what the page is for

Exactly one H1 per page, and the outline beneath it steps H2 → H3 without ever skipping a level — so a crawler reads the page the way you'd read a contents list. Headings carry the words buyers type, not slogans that only make sense after you've bought. verify: 22 pages, one H1 each

A photo labelled to be found

The filename is a search phrase in its own right — who, trade, town. The alt text describes what is honestly in the frame, which serves screen-reader users and image search with the same sentence. And the dimensions are declared in the markup, so the page never shifts while it loads — a Core Web Vitals signal Google measures directly. The falcon, by contrast, carries alt="" — it is decoration, and an empty alt is how you tell a screen reader to skip it. Labelling everything is as wrong as labelling nothing. verify: right-click either image → inspect

Structured data: who, what, where

A machine-readable block that stops Google guessing: the business is a ProfessionalService, here are its services, and areaServed names the exact towns it wants to rank in. A Person entity ties the founder to the firm, and the Business Profile points back here — each corroborating the other. verify: search "schema validator" → paste the URL

Links that say where they go

Every internal link's anchor text describes its destination — "web design in Telford", never "click here" — so each link passes meaning as well as visitors. Breadcrumbs on every page ship with matching BreadcrumbList schema, which is why search results for this site can show a trail instead of a bare URL. verify: any page → the breadcrumb line

Common questions

Asked before, answered straight.

Why a three-month minimum?

Because SEO genuinely takes three to six months to show results, and a one-month trial proves nothing except that you spent a month's fee. Three months is the shortest honest test. After that it's monthly rolling — if it isn't earning its keep, you stop.

Which areas do you cover?

Telford and Shropshire, Wolverhampton, Birmingham and the wider West Midlands. There is a dedicated page for SEO in Telford.

Can you guarantee rankings?

No, and nobody honest can — Google's results aren't for sale to consultants. What I guarantee is the work: the deliverables listed in your tier, done every month, reported honestly. That's the part anyone can actually control.

What if my website is the problem?

Then I'll tell you before taking a retainer. SEO on a slow, broken site is pouring water into a cracked bucket — sometimes the right first step is a rebuild, and you'd rather hear that up front than three months in.

Do I keep the work if I leave?

Yes. The content, the citations, the profile improvements, the rankings — they're attached to your business, not to me. No hostages.
